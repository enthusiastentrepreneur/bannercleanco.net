Is It Time to Hire a Cleaner? Signs, Costs & What to Expect

By Banner Clean Co. · Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

☕ Article cover photo goes here 1200×675px JPG or WebP — someone relaxing in a tidy living room, or a bright entryway, works well

Most people don't decide to hire a cleaner in one moment. It builds up — a Saturday morning lost to hoovering instead of the things you'd rather be doing, a holiday-let review that mentions "a bit grubby," or a home that always feels one step behind, no matter how often you tidy it. If you're reading this, some part of you is already asking whether it's worth paying someone else to take it off your plate. Here's how to think it through.

Five Signs You're Ready for Help

  • Cleaning is eating your actual time off. If your weekend keeps getting swallowed by chores instead of rest, family, or work you're behind on, the "free" option isn't actually free.
  • You're cleaning right before people arrive, not on a schedule. Reactive cleaning (rushing before guests, viewings, or inspections) is usually a sign the underlying routine has stopped working.
  • A guest review has mentioned cleanliness. For a holiday let, this one is worth acting on immediately — more on why below.
  • You're moving, and the end-of-tenancy clean feels bigger than the move itself. These cleans are more thorough than routine upkeep, and they're the ones people most often underestimate.
  • You'd rather pay than do it — and you can. That's a perfectly good reason on its own. You don't need a bigger justification than "my time is worth more to me spent elsewhere."

For Holiday-Let Hosts: Cleanliness Is a Review Category, Not Just an Opinion

If you host on Airbnb or a similar platform, cleanliness isn't a vague impression guests form — it's one of the specific categories they rate you on directly, separate from location, communication, or value. Airbnb is explicit that these individual ratings factor into how a listing performs in search, which means a slipping cleanliness score doesn't just cost you one bad review — it can quietly cost you future bookings you never see.

That risk is highest exactly when you're busiest: back-to-back turnovers in peak season, with barely a gap between one guest leaving and the next arriving. It's also worth knowing that cleanliness is assessed directly as part of Ireland's own accommodation standards if you're pursuing Fáilte Ireland's quality-assured accommodation approval — so getting this right matters beyond guest reviews too.

What It Actually Costs to Hire a Cleaner in Co. Clare

This is usually the real question underneath everything else, so here's a straight answer rather than a vague "it depends":

  • A standard clean for a 2–3 bed home runs €65 — cheaper than most people guess.
  • A holiday-let turnover clean for the same size starts at €55, or €62 bundled with a linen change.
  • A full deep clean, the kind you'd normally set aside a whole day for, starts at €80 for a smaller home.
  • End-of-tenancy cleaning, usually the most involved job, starts at €95.

Weigh that against the actual afternoon it takes to do properly yourself, and for a lot of people the maths tips toward hiring it out well before they expect it to.

One-Off or Regular? Picking the Right Clean

If you're not sure what to book, this is roughly how our clients sort themselves out:

  • Standard Clean — the everyday reset: kitchen, bathroom, floors, surfaces. Good as a one-off or on a regular schedule.
  • Deep Clean — for a spring clean, before guests or family visit, or when it's been a while since anything got real attention.
  • Holiday-Let Turnover — a fast, guest-ready reset between bookings, timed around your check-out/check-in window.
  • End-of-Tenancy — the thorough, everything-checked clean for moving in or out.
